## Ruleset version

- The current official Ontario amateur MMA ruleset is Version 2.0, effective May 27, 2025 (page last updated June 2, 2025). No newer version was found on 2026-10-17. Quote: "Version: 2.0" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts. Confidence: high.

## Ruleset legal basis

- O. Reg. 565/24 s. 2(1) para 10 makes the published MMA amateur rules binding, 'as they may be amended from time to time'. The Act says these rules apply even to events that need no permit. Quote: "The conduct of amateur combative sport contests and exhibitions is subject to the rules set out in the following documents" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240565. Confidence: high.

## Sanctioning

- Every amateur MMA event in Ontario must be sanctioned by the OAC. None of the 10 recognized provincial sport organizations covers MMA, so there is no PSO route. Quote: "All amateur MMA contests or exhibitions in Ontario must be sanctioned by the Ontario Athletics Commission." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts. Confidence: high.

## Age categories (MMA)

- Senior is 19+, Youth A is 16-18, Youth B is 14-15 and Youth C is 12-13. The ruleset has no MMA category for anyone under 12. Quote: "Senior: 19 years and over" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-i-general. Confidence: high.

## Youth MMA: no head strikes

- Under-18 amateur MMA is allowed, but only under youth rules: no strikes to the head or face at any youth level. Note that Youth A runs to age 18, so an 18-year-old cannot be struck in the head. Quote: "Striking to the head or face is not permitted for any and all levels of Youth A, B, and C." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-iii-technical-sport-rules. Confidence: high.

## Experience classes

- Pre-Novice means 5 or fewer verifiable amateur combat-sport contests (any combat sport). Novice means 6-10 and Open means 11 or more. Quote: "Pre-Novice Amateur Class: Any contestant who has five or less completed verifiable amateur combative sport contests." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-i-general. Confidence: high.

## Cross-class matching

- Opponents must be in the same experience class unless the Commissioner approves a cross-class bout. Quote: "No contestant shall compete against an opponent that is outside of their class unless approved by the Commissioner." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-i-general. Confidence: high.

## Competitive matchmaking attestation

- The permit application must attest that every bout is competitive and that matchmaking follows the prescribed rules. Quote: "An attestation from the applicant that all bouts will be competitive" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Amateur status: prize value

- A fighter becomes a professional if the purse, prize, ticket share or other consideration is or could be worth $300 or more. Belts, trophies, medals and reasonable travel and living expenses do not count. Keep any amateur prizes under $300. Quote: "is or could be $300 or greater" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Amateur status: past pro activity

- A fighter who holds or held a pro licence, or who fought as a pro anywhere, is a professional unless the Commissioner reinstated them as an amateur. Reinstatement requires 3 or fewer pro bouts, no more than 1 win, no pro bout in the last 6 months, and is one-time only. An amateur who faces such a fighter becomes a professional contestant too. Quote: "The contestant has participated in a combative sport contest or exhibition in any jurisdiction as a professional contestant" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Weigh-in timing (single-bout club show)

- For a club show, fighters weigh in on the day of the bout, no more than 3 hours before the first bout. Day-before weigh-ins are not allowed. Quote: "shall weigh-in the day of the bout within 3 hours of the first bout." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-i-general. Confidence: high.

## Weigh-in allowance

- An overweight fighter gets 1 hour to make the contracted weight. If they lose more than 1% of their weigh-in body weight during that hour, they cannot fight. Quote: "shall be allowed one hour to make the weight required by their agreement" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-i-general. Confidence: high.

## Max weight difference between opponents (single bout)

- Maximum difference between opponents: 4 lb under 148 lb, 9 lb at 148-165 lb, 11 lb at 165-200 lb, 24 lb at 200-265 lb, and no limit over 265 lb. The bands overlap at exactly 165 and 200 lb. Quote: "4 lbs: under 148 lbs;" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-i-general. Confidence: high.

## Weigh-in timing (tournament)

- At a tournament, fighters weigh in each competition day. Weigh-ins start no more than 4 hours before competition and finish at least 2 hours before. Quote: "All weigh-ins must commence no more than 4 hours prior to the beginning of the competition" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-i-general. Confidence: high.

## Tournament divisions and catch weights

- Fixed weight divisions (listed in weight_classes) apply to tournaments, and catch weights may be added. In the upper Youth B and C class, opponents may differ by no more than 11 lb. Quote: "Catch weight divisions may be added at the time of the tournament." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-i-general. Confidence: high.

## Weigh-in location in application

- The permit application must give the weigh-in location and time if they differ from the event date and time. Quote: "The proposed weigh-in location and time if different from the date and time in paragraph 1." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Postponed bouts

- If a bout is postponed 24 hours or more, the fighters need a second weigh-in and a new pre-bout medical on the day of the contest. Quote: "a second weigh-in and an additional pre-bout medical examination shall be required on the day of the contest." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-i-general. Confidence: high.

## Rounds (Senior)

- Senior bouts are 3 rounds of 3 minutes with 1 minute of rest. The ruleset gives no different length for pre-novice or novice. Quote: "Each Senior bout will be 3 x 3-minute rounds with a 1-minute rest period." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-iii-technical-sport-rules. Confidence: high.

## Rounds (Youth)

- Youth A bouts are 3 rounds of 2 minutes with 1 minute of rest. Youth B and Youth C bouts are 2 rounds of 2 minutes. Quote: "Each Youth A bout will be 3 x 2-minute rounds with a 1-minute rest period." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-iii-technical-sport-rules. Confidence: high.

## Gloves

- Gloves are at least 6 oz, open-handed with finger loops, and visibly bigger and rounder than pro gloves. The promoter supplies them, every fighter wears the same type, and tape goes over the Velcro. Quote: "gloves with protective padding weighing a minimum of no less than 6 ounces" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-i-general. Confidence: high.

## Gloves supplied by promoter

- Fighters may not bring their own gloves. Quote: "Contestants are not permitted to supply their own gloves." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-i-general. Confidence: high.

## Hand wraps

- Per hand: gauze up to 2 in wide and 30-60 ft long, and tape up to 1 in wide and 15-30 ft long. The promoter supplies the materials and the people who wrap. An official approves the wraps before gloves go on. Quote: "The promoter must provide personnel to wrap hands." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-i-general. Confidence: high.

## Shin guards

- Shin guards are mandatory for every class. They must be soft neoprene, tight-fitting, and have no buckles, straps or exposed Velcro. Quote: "shall wear shin guards made of soft neoprene construction" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-iii-technical-sport-rules. Confidence: high.

## Groin / chest protection

- Men must wear a foul-proof groin protector. Women may wear a groin or breast protector but are not required to. Quote: "Contestants who are men shall wear a foul-proof groin protector." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-iii-technical-sport-rules. Confidence: high.

## Headgear, shoes, gi prohibited

- Headgear is not allowed, and neither are shoes, martial arts uniforms, or tape and braces other than those the ruleset allows. Quote: "headgear or any other similar protective equipment;" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-iii-technical-sport-rules. Confidence: high.

## Shorts and rash guards

- Shorts are MMA or compression shorts with no pockets, zips or fasteners and no external lace-up waistband. Rash guards are optional and must be short-sleeved, unless written permission is given for cultural or religious reasons. Quote: "all rash guards (if applicable) are to be of short sleeve type" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-iii-technical-sport-rules. Confidence: high.

## Mouthguard

- The apparel section does not list a mouthguard as mandatory. However, the ruleset assumes one: it covers dislodged mouthpieces, and dropping the mouthpiece counts as a timidity foul. Treat it as required and confirm with the OAC. Quote: "If a contestant’s mouthpiece is involuntarily dislodged during the progress of a round" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-iii-technical-sport-rules. Confidence: medium.

## Appearance

- Fighters must tie back hair, contain facial hair, trim nails, remove jewelry, dentures, glasses and hard contacts, and wear no cosmetics. A light coat of petroleum jelly is allowed only on the brows, under the eyes and on the nose. Quote: "remove all jewelry and accessories;" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-i-general. Confidence: high.

## Banned techniques: elbows

- Elbow and forearm strikes are fouls for every class. Quote: "elbow and forearm strikes of any kind to any area" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-iii-technical-sport-rules. Confidence: high.

## Banned techniques: knees to head

- Knees to the head are banned for every class, as are knees or kicks to the head of a grounded opponent and stomps. Quote: "kneeing the head of an opponent" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-iii-technical-sport-rules. Confidence: high.

## Banned techniques: leg locks / cranks

- Twisting leg locks such as heel hooks and toe holds are banned, while straight kneebars and straight ankle locks are allowed. Also banned: neck cranks, spine locks, small-joint manipulation, linear thrusting kicks to the front of the knee, pile-drivers and strikes to the spine or back of the head. Quote: "all twisting leg submissions such as heel hooks and toe holds (straight kneebars and straight ankle locks are allowed)" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-iii-technical-sport-rules. Confidence: high.

## Pre-Novice extra fouls

- Pre-Novice also bans strikes to the head of a grounded opponent, suplexes, elevated or shoulder throws, slams, up-kicks from the ground and foot stomps. Quote: "no suplex, elevated/shoulder throws or slams" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-iii-technical-sport-rules. Confidence: high.

## Novice extra foul

- Novice also bans strikes to the head of a grounded opponent. Open class has no extra restrictions. Quote: "no striking the head of a grounded opponent" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-iii-technical-sport-rules. Confidence: high.

## Youth extra fouls

- All youth levels ban knee strikes, bicep and calf crushes, wristlocks, slams and up-kicks. Youth B and C also ban all ground strikes and the omoplata. Youth C bans all leg submissions and all guillotines. Youth A and B allow only arm-in guillotines finished on the ground. Quote: "no striking of any kind with the knees" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-iii-technical-sport-rules. Confidence: high.

## Annual medical (ruleset)

- Each fighter needs a physical exam by an Ontario-licensed physician covering the competitive calendar year. Quote: "completed a physical examination conducted by an Ontario licenced physician for the competitive calendar year." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-i-general. Confidence: high.

## Annual medical (regulation)

- When the rules allow full-contact head strikes, as MMA does, each fighter must give the permit holder proof of a medical exam done no more than 1 year before the event that says they are fit to compete. Quote: "no more than one year before the date of the amateur combative sport contest or exhibition" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Pre-bout medical

- An Ontario-licensed physician must examine each fighter no later than 1 hour before their bout. Quote: "completed no later than one hour before the bout." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-i-general. Confidence: high.

## Other medical tests

- Bloodwork (HIV, hepatitis, syphilis), a dilated eye exam, an ECG and brain imaging are required only for professional licence applicants. Nothing equivalent was found for amateurs in the regulation or the MMA ruleset. Quote: "to detect the presence of antibodies to the Human Immunodeficiency Virus" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: medium.

## Signed codes

- Each fighter must sign the Athlete Code of Conduct, the Concussion Code of Conduct and a rules declaration. Quote: "Each contestant must have signed the Athlete Code of Conduct, Concussion Code of Conduct, and rules declaration." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-i-general. Confidence: high.

## Concussion awareness

- The promoter must attest that every fighter has reviewed concussion awareness resources and that a removal-from-sport and return-to-play protocol is followed. Quote: "An attestation that the applicant will ensure that all contestants have reviewed concussion awareness resources" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Licences not required for amateurs

- Under the Act, licences are only needed for professional promoters, contestants, managers, matchmakers, seconds and officials. Amateur fighters and corners need no OAC licence; the promoter needs an event permit instead. Quote: "Participate as a contestant in a professional combative sport contest or exhibition." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/19c07b. Confidence: high.

## Contestant ID

- No contestant photo-ID rule was found for amateurs; photo ID is required only from the permit applicant. Age categories still mean ages must be verified. Quote: "a copy of valid photo identification showing age or date of birth for each person required to be named" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: medium.

## Day-of suspension attestation

- On the event date, every fighter completes a Commissioner-approved attestation that they have no suspension, prohibition or limitation from any other combat-sport organization. Quote: "confirming that the contestant does not have a suspension, prohibition or other limitation in effect" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Minors: parent/guardian attestation

- For a fighter under 18, a parent or guardian completes the day-of suspension attestation. Quote: "shall be completed by the contestant’s parent or guardian." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Amateur books

- The permit holder must keep 'amateur books' for every fighter, recording suspensions, injuries, results and limitations from its own event and from other organizations. Quote: "The permit holder must maintain amateur books of contestant suspensions, injuries, results of contests" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## No suspended fighters

- The permit holder must check each fighter's amateur book and must not let a suspended fighter compete. Quote: "review each contestant’s amateur book and not allow a contestant on suspension to compete." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Medical suspension length (pro benchmark only)

- No amateur KO/TKO suspension periods are published. For licensed pros, a fighter cannot compete within 60 days of any KO or TKO, or after 2 head KOs/TKOs in 6 months or 3 in 1 year. These periods could serve as a conservative benchmark for amateurs. Quote: "in the 60-day period preceding the date of the contest or exhibition" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: medium.

## Official record / registry

- No official amateur registry was found. The regulation names an ABC-recognized record keeper only for professional results and suspensions. Sherdog and Tapology are not official. For amateurs, checks rely on the fighter's attestation, the amateur books and inquiries to other organizations. Quote: "keeping records of bout results and suspensions of professional combative sport contestants" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: medium.

## Officials required per bout

- No bout may start without a referee, a ringside medical practitioner seated near the steps, 3 judges on separate sides, a timekeeper and a scorekeeper. Quote: "No bout of Amateur Mixed Martial Arts shall begin without:" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-iii-technical-sport-rules. Confidence: high.

## Judging

- Three judges score every round on the 10-point must system, with no fractions; an even round is 10-10. Quote: "All bouts shall be scored by three judges." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-iii-technical-sport-rules. Confidence: high.

## Timekeeper kit

- The timekeeper needs 2 stopwatches, a horn, a wooden clapper (10-second warning in rounds) and a whistle (10-second warning in rest periods). Quote: "at least two accurate stop watches or similar devices for keeping time;" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-iii-technical-sport-rules. Confidence: high.

## Pre-bout equipment inspection

- An official inspects each fighter's appearance, apparel and equipment just before they enter the cage or ring. Quote: "An official shall inspect each contestants appearance, apparel and equipment immediately prior to their entry" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-iii-technical-sport-rules. Confidence: high.

## Rules briefing

- Before each bout, the referee briefs each fighter and their corner in the dressing room and identifies the lead second. No separate event-wide rules meeting was found. Quote: "the referee shall give instructions on the rules and conduct of the bout to each contestant and their seconds in the dressing room." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-i-general. Confidence: high.

## Seconds (corners)

- A fighter may have up to 3 seconds, or 4 for a championship bout. Two may enter between rounds, and the promoter supplies a neutral third second for cuts. Seconds stay seated during rounds and leave at the 10-second whistle. Quote: "three seconds, for all other bouts." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-i-general. Confidence: high.

## Corner materials

- Only these items are allowed in the corner: petroleum jelly, enswells, cotton swabs, gauze pads, bottled water, a clear water bottle, a clear spray bottle, bagged ice and a bucket, towels, sponges, athletic tape and bandage scissors. Quote: "The only materials which may be present in a contestant’s corner for use by a second are:" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-i-general. Confidence: high.

## Corner stoppage

- If the lead second asks the referee to stop the bout, their fighter loses by TKO. Quote: "the lead second’s contestant shall be declared the loser of the bout by technical knockout." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-i-general. Confidence: high.

## Dressing rooms

- The promoter must provide suitable dressing rooms for fighters and officials, plus a separate room for post-bout medical exams. Quote: "The promoter must provide suitable dressing rooms for the contestants and officials." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-i-general. Confidence: high.

## Post-bout medical

- After the result is announced, the fighter and at least one second go promptly to the post-bout exam room. Quote: "shall promptly report to the room designated for a post-bout examination by a ringside medical practitioner." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-i-general. Confidence: high.

## Physician present

- Because MMA allows full-contact head strikes, a CPSO-licensed physician must be on site for the entire event. Quote: "A physician licensed and in good standing with the College of Physicians and Surgeons of Ontario must be present at all times" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Onsite medical team

- At least 2 onsite medical personnel must be present for the whole event. One must be a physician or nurse practitioner (for MMA, a physician). The other can be a physician, NP, RN, paramedic, EMS paramedic, CATA athletic therapist or WSIB-recognized first aider. Quote: "at least one other individual who meets a requirement set out in paragraphs 1 to 7" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Simultaneous bouts

- If bouts run at the same time, such as grappling mats beside the cage, each one needs at least one medical person close by. Quote: "at least one member of the onsite medical personnel in close proximity to each bout." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Ambulance

- The dedicated ambulance with 2 paramedics is a term of professional permits only. The amateur regulation requires onsite medical personnel and an emergency plan, not an ambulance, though the Commissioner can add permit conditions. Quote: "a municipal off-call or special events ambulance and two paramedics" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: medium.

## Commissioner can add conditions

- The OAC can attach extra conditions to any permit, so read the permit letter itself. Quote: "may impose any other terms and conditions that the Commissioner considers appropriate." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/19c07b. Confidence: high.

## Cage size

- The cage must be round or have at least 6 equal sides and be 18-32 ft wide. It needs at least 6 padded metal posts, a vinyl-coated fence (or similar), steps at 2 opposite entrances, and padding over all metal. Quote: "must be at least 18 feet wide and no larger than 32 feet wide" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-ii-competition-area. Confidence: high.

## Cage/ring platform height

- The cage or ring platform must be raised 3 to 4 ft above the floor, with at least 1 in of padding and a taut canvas or vinyl cover. A floor-level cage would not meet this. Quote: "be elevated at least three feet but not more than four feet above the floor of the building" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-ii-competition-area. Confidence: high.

## Ring option

- A ring is the alternative: 18-24 ft square inside the ropes, a 2 ft apron, 5 padded ropes, and a canvas cover (not vinyl). Quote: "The ring shall not be less than 18 feet nor more than 24 feet square between the ring ropes" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-ii-competition-area. Confidence: high.

## Venue layout

- Lights must be mounted at least 14 ft up, with nothing within 14 ft above the competition area. Officials sit at least 6 ft from spectators, and the area is secured from the crowd. Quote: "at least six feet from spectators" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-ii-competition-area. Confidence: high.

## Promoter-supplied equipment

- The promoter provides a stool, seconds' chairs and a clean bucket for each corner; stools for judges; tables for medical, timekeeper and scorekeeper; a horn or bell; and bottled water. It also needs an attendant on hand for repairs and attendants to close cage doors and clean after each bout. Quote: "a stool for use during the rest period between rounds;" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-ii-competition-area. Confidence: high.

## Bout result types

- Recognized results: submission (tap, verbal tap, technical), KO, TKO, unanimous/split/majority decision, unanimous/majority/split draw, technical decision, technical draw, no contest and DQ. Quote: "Technical knockout, if:" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-iii-technical-sport-rules. Confidence: high.

## Video recording

- If the promoter records the event, the application must include an agreement to give the Commissioner an electronic recording. Quote: "the applicant’s agreement to provide the Commissioner with an electronic recording of the event." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Permit signage

- Post the Commissioner-provided permit sign at the main entrance or another conspicuous spot. Quote: "signage in a form provided by the Commissioner that identifies that a permit has been issued" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Post-event report

- Within 14 days, file the post-event report (form ON00735) with total contestants, total bouts, anonymized injury data, a list of officials and anything else the Commissioner requires. Quote: "no later than 14 days after the contest or exhibition" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Inspections

- OAC inspectors may enter any amateur event without a warrant and examine records. Violations can lead to compliance orders and administrative penalties of up to $10,000, or more where there was an economic benefit. Quote: "enter and inspect" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/19c07b. Confidence: high.

## Offences

- Breaking the Act or the regulations is an offence: up to $10,000 and/or 2 years for an individual, or up to $100,000 for a corporation. Quote: "a fine of not more than $10,000 or to imprisonment for a term of not more than two years" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/19c07b. Confidence: high.

## Youth grappling: regulated, not exempt

- The Act defines combative sport to include grappling and submissions, and Ontario's grappling ruleset rates grappling 'Full contact'. The light-contact exemption therefore does not apply. Youth grappling needs either OAC permit coverage or sanction or permission from the Ontario Grappling Association. Quote: "Full contact." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-grappling/part-i-overview. Confidence: medium.

## Grappling sanctioning

- Amateur grappling must be sanctioned by the OAC or by the Ontario Grappling Association, the recognized PSO. Quote: "must be sanctioned by the Ontario Athletics Commission (OAC) or the Ontario Grappling Association." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-grappling. Confidence: high.

## PSO exemption

- No OAC permit is needed if a recognized PSO holds the event or gives permission for it, for example the OGA for grappling. The prescribed rules still apply. Quote: "held with the permission of a provincial sports organization recognized by the Ministry" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Grappling ages and allowed moves

- The grappling ruleset has kids divisions from age 4. Allowed submissions by age: straight armbar from 7, kimura/americana from 9, RNC (and no-gi triangle) from 11, most chokes and foot locks from 16. Heel hooks, neck cranks, slams and all striking are never allowed. Quote: "Kids 4–6 years old" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-grappling/part-v-participants. Confidence: high.

## Grappling weigh-ins

- At a single-bout event, grappling weigh-ins start no more than 3 hours before the event and close at least 60 minutes before. Parents or guardians may accompany junior athletes. Quote: "weigh-ins take place no more than 3 hours before the start of the event" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-grappling/part-iii-pre-competition-requirements. Confidence: high.

## Grappling weight classes

- Grappling has its own weight classes from 40 lb to over 265 lb, each with a 2 lb allowance. Quote: "40 Pounds + (2 pounds weight allowance)" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-grappling/part-v-participants. Confidence: high.

## Grappling match length

- Regulation bouts are 3 rounds of 3 minutes with 1 minute of rest. Non-championship matches for qualifying or skill-building may be a single match of 2-10 minutes. Quote: "Regulation bouts are 3 x 3-minute rounds, with a 1-minute rest between rounds." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-grappling/part-iv-competition-requirements. Confidence: high.

## Grappling matchmaking

- Match by experience and number of matches: beginner under 1 year, intermediate 1-3 years, advanced 3+ years. Quote: "Beginner: Less than 1 year of training." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-grappling/part-v-participants. Confidence: high.

## Grappling attire

- No-gi athletes wear rash guards, shorts or spats and may wear mouth guards; cups are not permitted. Gis must be one colour (white, royal blue, black or red). Quote: "Cups/Jocks are not permitted." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-grappling/part-iv-competition-requirements. Confidence: medium.

## Rowan's Law (grappling and youth)

- Rowan's Law lists grappling, jiu-jitsu, wrestling, boxing and kickboxing as amateur competitive sports; MMA is not listed. An organizer running grappling competitions with anyone under 26 likely counts as a 'sport organization'. Quote: "The organization has at least one individual under 26 years of age who participates" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/190161. Confidence: medium.

## Rowan's Law duties

- A sport organization must collect confirmations that athletes reviewed concussion resources (parents too for under-18s), with confirmations also from coaches and officials. It must also have a concussion code of conduct and removal-from-sport and return-to-sport protocols. Quote: "the individual’s parent or guardian must also give the sport organization confirmation" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/18r01. Confidence: high.

## Rowan's Law removal

- An athlete suspected of a concussion is removed immediately and returns only through the return-to-sport protocol. Quote: "immediately removed from further training, practice or competition if the athlete is suspected of having sustained a concussion" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/18r01. Confidence: high.

## Permit required

- Holding an amateur combat-sport event without a valid event permit is illegal unless an exemption applies (light contact only, PSO-held or PSO-permitted, or school wrestling). Quote: "unless the person holds a valid event permit authorizing the contest or exhibition." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/19c07b. Confidence: high.

## Venue owner duty

- The venue owner or operator must not allow the event unless the OAC has issued a permit for that venue. Quote: "unless the Commissioner has issued a valid event permit for the contest or exhibition to be held in or at that building" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/19c07b. Confidence: high.

## Criminal Code link

- The OAC permit is what makes an amateur event count as permitted by the Lieutenant Governor in Council under Criminal Code s. 83 (prize fights). Quote: "is deemed to be permission to hold the contest or exhibition by the Lieutenant Governor in Council" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/19c07b. Confidence: high.

## Permit timing

- Confirmed: apply at least 30 days before a single or tournament event. With a multi-event permit, file 30 days before each event. Quote: "at least 30 days before the start date of the proposed event." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Late applications

- The Commissioner may still consider an application filed 14-29 days before the event, at their discretion. Quote: "less than 30 days but no less than 14 days before the date of the proposed event." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Permit fees

- Confirmed and non-refundable: $600 for a single contest or exhibition, $1,600 for a tournament-style event, $3,000 for up to eight single contests or exhibitions. Quote: "$600 for an event permit to hold a single amateur combative sport contest or exhibition." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## What 'contest' means (one card)

- A 'contest or exhibition' is the whole event: one bout or several consecutive bouts, in one or more sports. So one $600 permit should cover a full card of 8-12 MMA bouts plus youth grappling. The 'eight' in the $3,000 permit means 8 events, not 8 bouts. Quote: "composed of a single bout in a combative sport or two or more consecutive bouts in one or more combative sports." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: medium.

## Multi-event permit (more than 8)

- A multi-event permit covers at most 8 events and expires 1 year after issue or at the last event, whichever is first. A 9th event, or any after expiry, needs a new permit. Before each event, file a 30-day notice with the date, venue, emergency plan, medical list, code of conduct, officials, venue letter and insurance. Quote: "A maximum of eight amateur combative sport contests or exhibitions may be held under the permit." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Ruleset changes: 120 days

- Confirmed: requests to modify or be exempted from the prescribed rules, and new-sport rulesets, go to the Minister at least 120 days before the event (via AthleticsComm@ontario.ca). Quote: "at least 120 days before the contest or exhibition is to commence" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240565. Confidence: high.

## Application: officials

- The application must include officials' qualification and selection policies and name each official, with the number and type of events each has worked in the last 24 months. Quote: "The names of each official participating in the contest, including the number and types of events the official has officiated in the last 24 months." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Application: emergency plan

- The emergency plan must name the person who coordinates emergencies and include an infectious disease and blood policy, an injury assessment and removal-from-play plan, an attestation that emergency contacts are collected, and a medical kit list. Quote: "the name of a designated individual responsible for supervising or coordinating any emergency response" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Application: code of conduct

- Submit a code of conduct covering athletes, officials and spectators, including consequences for misconduct and a dispute process. Quote: "A copy of a code of conduct governing the contest or exhibition" Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Insurance certificate

- The application needs a commercial general liability certificate of at least $2,000,000 per occurrence, extended to cover referees, judges, officials and the Commissioner. Quote: "Minimum coverage of $2,000,000 for each occurrence covered by the insurance." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Application form

- The permit application is form ON00734 (version ON00734E 2026/08). OAC contact: AthleticsComm@ontario.ca, 416-326-0416. Quote: "ON00734E (2026/08)" Source: https://www.sus.gov.on.ca/lc/content/mgcs/profiles/default.html?contentRoot=repository:///Applications/ON00734/1.0/Assets&template=ON00734E.xdp&submitUrl=https://localhost:12443/rest/services/ON00734/Processes/HTML/SubmitForm&lang=E&submitServiceProxy=https://www.sus.gov.on.ca/sub-proxy/all. Confidence: high.

## Purses

- Purses are not allowed for amateurs. Any purse, prize or ticket share worth $300 or more turns the fighter into a professional; trophies, belts and medals are fine. Quote: "A prize does not include a belt, trophy medal or similar award of achievement." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: high.

## Ticket / attendance reporting

- No ticket or attendance reporting rule was found for amateur permits. The post-event report covers contestants, bouts, injuries and officials. Only professional permit applications ask for venue seating capacity. Quote: "The total number of contestants who participated in the contest." Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/240507. Confidence: medium.

## Alcohol

- The combative-sports Act and regulations have no alcohol rule. Under AGCO rules, alcohol sold or served outside a licensed establishment needs a Special Occasion Permit; indoor public events must apply at least 30 days ahead. Quote: "A Special Occasion Permit (SOP) is required any time liquor is sold or served anywhere other than in a licensed establishment." Source: https://www.agco.ca/en/alcohol/guides/special-occasion-permit-guide/section-1-special-occasion-permits. Confidence: medium.
