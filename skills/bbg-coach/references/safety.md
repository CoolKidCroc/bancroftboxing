# Safety for BBG classes

## BBG house rules (Cody's; never loosen these)

- **Who decides** (Everyone): Cody, the head coach at every class, decides what's taught, who spars and who's matched with whom. These plans and drills are starting points.
- **Head contact for kids** (Under 12): No strikes to the head in class. Kids boxing is pads, bags, footwork and games; partner work is glove taps or cooperative drilling. (Boxing Canada lets coach-led conditioned sparring start gradually from about age 9-12; Cody decides if and when, with a certified coach.)
- **Sparring** (Everyone): Sparring with head contact only in the Competition Class, with Cody's OK, full gear (headgear, mouthguard, gloves) and a coach watching. MMA: no head or face strikes for anyone under 19 (Ontario youth MMA rules, ages 12-18).
- **Wrestling and grappling holds** (Kids and youth): Never: slams, back-bending throws, full or three-quarter nelsons, both hands on the neck, neck cranks, heel hooks. Throws only once breakfalls are solid. Youth submissions follow Ontario's no-gi table: straight armbar from 7, kimura from 9, rear naked choke and triangle from 11, the rest from 16.
- **After a hit to the head** (Everyone): Any sign of a concussion (headache, dizzy, confused, sick, unsteady): remove them from training right away and don't let them back in that day. Tell a parent for anyone under 18 and tell Cody. Only a doctor or nurse practitioner can clear them to return, and they follow the return-to-sport steps.
- **Designated person** (Every class): One named person at every class can remove an athlete with a suspected concussion. They've reviewed the concussion awareness resource in the last 12 months (Making Headway recommended). They don't diagnose. At BBG it's Cody for every class (Oct 2026).
- **Registration and coaches (Rowan's Law)** (Under 26): Before registering, athletes under 26 and parents of under-18s confirm they've read Ontario's concussion awareness resource (within 12 months) and the Concussion Code of Conduct. Coaches confirm both every year. BBG has this in place (Cody, Oct 2026).
- **Coach screening** (Coaches): Every BBG coach has a vulnerable sector check; some hold NCCP certification (Cody, Oct 2026). New coaches and helpers get a vulnerable sector check before they work with anyone under 18.
- **Rule of Two** (Kids and youth): Two screened adults present (a coach plus another coach, staff or parent). No coach alone one-on-one with a child, out of sight. No private messages to minors: group messages that include parents.
- **Ages 4-5** (Youngest kids): Cody takes kids from about 4. Keep it short and playful: footwork games, stance, punching pads a coach holds, the bag, relay races. No partner contact and no live games, a second adult present, water and rest often.
- **Mixed-age classes** (All-ages classes): In an all-ages class (Boxing, Mon and Wed), split into age groups for any drill above the youngest age: younger kids do footwork, pads a coach holds, the bag or a game while older athletes partner up. Never pair a child with a teen or adult for partner or touch drills.
- **Equipment** (Coaches): BBG has heavy bags, focus mitts, Thai pads and kick shields, wrestling mats, gloves to lend, headgear, agility ladders and cones, medicine balls and battle ropes (Cody, Oct 2026). No ring, cage or skipping ropes. Plans only use this gear.
- **Warm-up** (Everyone): Every class starts with a 10-15 minute warm-up that builds from easy jogging to balance, landing and quick footwork. Water breaks in every class.
- **Injuries and health** (Everyone): Coaches don't give medical advice and nobody writes health details in any sheet. Injuries go to Cody the same day.
- **Matching partners and the tap** (Everyone): Match by size, age and experience; a coach watches every pair in live rounds and stops anything unsafe. Tap early, let go at once: practise the tap with every beginner before any live grappling.

## What the sport bodies and Ontario say

Researched 2026-10-17. Confirm anything that matters with the source; this isn't legal or medical advice.

### Boxing LTAD stages

- (Boys 6-9 / girls 6-8) FUNdamentals: fundamental movement and basic sport skills with exposure to many sports; Boxing Canada suggests 1-2 'introduction to boxing' sessions of 45-75 minutes per week alongside 4-6 other activity sessions. [Boxing Canada (CABA) Long-Term Athlete Development guide (hosted by Boxing Ontario)](https://boxingontario.com/wp-content/uploads/Boxing-Canada-LTAD.pdf)
- (Boys 6-9 / girls 6-8) No maximal or near-maximal strength work at the FUNdamentals stage (growth-plate protection); equipment must be adapted to the child's size, strength and skill; focus on fun games and spatial awareness. [Boxing Canada (CABA) Long-Term Athlete Development guide (hosted by Boxing Ontario)](https://boxingontario.com/wp-content/uploads/Boxing-Canada-LTAD.pdf)
- (Boys 9-12 / girls 8-11) Learn to Box: basic boxing skills (straight punches, hooks, uppercuts, blocking, footwork) with technical precision; endurance and speed trained through fun games; 'children are not miniature adults'. [Boxing Canada (CABA) Long-Term Athlete Development guide (hosted by Boxing Ontario)](https://boxingontario.com/wp-content/uploads/Boxing-Canada-LTAD.pdf)
- (Boys 12-16 / girls 11-15) Train to Box: build physical preparation and boxing-specific skills, volume and intensity increase gradually; carefully selected competition, roughly 4-10 bouts per year in club events and junior tournaments. [Boxing Canada (CABA) Long-Term Athlete Development guide (hosted by Boxing Ontario)](https://boxingontario.com/wp-content/uploads/Boxing-Canada-LTAD.pdf)

### Contact / sparring for kids

- (Boys 9-12 / girls 8-11) Boxing Canada says coach-led 'simulated bouts, condition sparring, or shadow boxing' can be introduced progressively at Learn to Box; inter-club competition may be introduced for fun but should not be the main focus. [Boxing Canada (CABA) Long-Term Athlete Development guide (hosted by Boxing Ontario)](https://boxingontario.com/wp-content/uploads/Boxing-Canada-LTAD.pdf)

### Competition age and volume

- (11-12+) Boxing Canada LTAD indicates athletes may start competing at age 11, limited to about 2-4 well-structured club events per year against similar size and skill opponents (table context in the PDF was not fully verifiable). [Boxing Canada (CABA) Long-Term Athlete Development guide (hosted by Boxing Ontario)](https://boxingontario.com/wp-content/uploads/Boxing-Canada-LTAD.pdf)

### Session frequency/length

- (11+ (Learn to Box) and Train to Box) LTAD summary: 2-3 boxing-specific sessions per week of 45-75 min at the earlier stage; 3-5 sessions of 45-90 min at Train to Box (boys 12-16, girls 11-15), plus other sport/activity sessions. [Boxing Canada (CABA) Long-Term Athlete Development guide (hosted by Boxing Ontario)](https://boxingontario.com/wp-content/uploads/Boxing-Canada-LTAD.pdf)

### Growth spurt precautions

- (Boys 12-16 / girls 11-15) Injury risk rises during the growth spurt: adjust programs, address flexibility and strength imbalances, reduce volume/intensity; include shoulder and low-back injury prevention and 6-8 weeks of general conditioning before intense boxing-specific work. [Boxing Canada (CABA) Long-Term Athlete Development guide (hosted by Boxing Ontario)](https://boxingontario.com/wp-content/uploads/Boxing-Canada-LTAD.pdf)

### Work:rest for speed

- (All youth) Speed efforts should be very short (under 5 seconds for younger athletes, extendable to about 10-15 s later) with complete recovery between reps and sets; train speed early in the session after a thorough warm-up. No explicit work:rest ratio is given. [Boxing Canada (CABA) Long-Term Athlete Development guide (hosted by Boxing Ontario)](https://boxingontario.com/wp-content/uploads/Boxing-Canada-LTAD.pdf)

### Trainability windows

- (Kids and teens) Flexibility window is about ages 6-10 for both sexes; speed windows about 6-8 and 11-13 (girls), 7-9 and 13-16 (boys); emphasize aerobic endurance through continuous training during the growth spurt. [Boxing Canada (CABA) Long-Term Athlete Development guide (hosted by Boxing Ontario)](https://boxingontario.com/wp-content/uploads/Boxing-Canada-LTAD.pdf)

### Youth competition categories

- (6-18) Boxing Ontario club events: Fun Box ages 6-10 (U13 may also take part), U13 = 11/12, U15 = 13/14, U17 = 15/16, U19 = 17/18; age matching generally within 12 months. [Boxing Ontario - Match Guidelines, Club Events 2025](https://boxingontario.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Match-Guidelines-Club-Events-2025.pdf)

### Round lengths for youth

- (U13 / U15 / U17) Boxing Ontario 2025 club events: U13 3 x 1 min, U15 3 x 1.5 min, U17 3 x 2 min; boxers may box fewer rounds but not more. Ontario rules set a 1-minute rest between rounds. [Boxing Ontario - Match Guidelines, Club Events 2025](https://boxingontario.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Match-Guidelines-Club-Events-2025.pdf)

### Headgear and gloves (youth)

- (Youth and women) Open-face headgear is mandatory for all in Boxing Ontario club events (cheek-protector headgear allowed only for Fun Box and Masters if both wear the same type); 10 oz gloves for females, U17, U15 and U13 bouts. [Boxing Ontario - Match Guidelines, Club Events 2025](https://boxingontario.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Match-Guidelines-Club-Events-2025.pdf)

### Fun Box age range (provincial rules)

- (6-12) Ontario's official amateur boxing ruleset lists 'Initiation Funbox' as 6-12 years, Junior A U13, Junior B U15, Junior C U17; note this differs from Boxing Ontario's 2025 club guideline (6-10). [Ontario Amateur Combative Sport Rules: Boxing - Part V Participants](https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-boxing/part-v-participants)

### Protective equipment (competition)

- (All) Ontario boxing rules: headgear mandatory for all sanctioned competitions except National Championships; gumshield required every bout; men must wear a groin protector, women may. [Ontario Amateur Combative Sport Rules: Boxing - Part IV Competition requirements](https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-boxing/part-iv-competition-requirements)

### Supervision of sparring

- (All) Boxing Ontario: registered, Boxing Ontario certified coaches must be present to supervise any training/sparring; a registered club needs at least one NCCP Instruction Beginner coach. [Boxing Ontario - Becoming a Registered Club](https://boxingontario.com/clubs/becoming-a-registered-club-with-boxing-ontario/)

### Insurance

- (All) Boxing Ontario liability insurance covers only Boxing Ontario members in membership-related activities, so participants must be registered members to be covered. [Boxing Ontario - Becoming a Registered Club](https://boxingontario.com/clubs/becoming-a-registered-club-with-boxing-ontario/)

### Non-contact youth boxing

- (8-16) Boxing Ontario's BOX-ON! is a standardized non-contact boxing-fitness program for youth 8-16 that any club or youth centre can adapt (article undated; check whether still offered). *(low confidence: check)* [Boxing Ontario - BOX-ON! non-contact youth program article (year not stated)](https://boxingontario.com/?p=16257)

### Paediatric position on boxing

- (Children and teens) The Canadian Paediatric Society (2011) said boxing is not appropriate for children and teens and recommended sports where deliberate head blows are not the focus; the statement is dated and its current status is unclear. [Canadian Paediatric Society - 'Boxing not for children and teens' (2011, updated 2012)](https://cps.ca/en/media/Boxing-not-for-children-and-teens)

### Wrestling LTAD stages

- (6-9) FUNdamentals: structured play in fun, safe environments with minimal competition emphasis; non-aggressive tumbling and play-wrestling; modified equipment; speed activities under 5 seconds. [Wrestling Canada Lutte - LTAD Manual (2015, still the posted version)](https://wrestling.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/LTAD_Manual_2015.pdf)
- (10-12) Learning to Wrestle: core principles, basic takedowns and par terre; use games and simulations and avoid complex tactics, highly repetitive drills and high-intensity physical training; 1+ festival-style events per year with modified rules. [Wrestling Canada Lutte - LTAD Manual (2015, still the posted version)](https://wrestling.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/LTAD_Manual_2015.pdf)
- (12-16) Training to Compete (Bantam/Cadet): core skill repetition, off-mat cross-training, tactics; about 10-20 matches per year; avoid cutting late maturers and pushing early maturers too hard, too soon. [Wrestling Canada Lutte - LTAD Seven Stages](https://wrestling.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/LTAD_E.pdf)

### Kids physiology

- (6-9) Wrestling Canada notes children have low anaerobic capacity and limited ability to regulate body temperature in extreme heat or cold. [Wrestling Canada Lutte - LTAD Manual (2015, still the posted version)](https://wrestling.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/LTAD_Manual_2015.pdf)

### Youth age groups

- (7-14) OAWA youth age groups: Tykes 7/8, Novices 9/10, Kids 11/12, Bantam 13/14 (age as of Dec 31); youth rules use Wrestling Canada U17 rules with OAWA modifications. [Ontario Amateur Wrestling Association - Youth Wrestling Rules](https://www.oawa.ca/youth-wrestling-rules)

### Banned moves - all age-group wrestling

- (7-14) Illegal for all age-group wrestlers in Ontario: slams, all back-bending throws (side suplay, salto), full and 3/4 nelson, both hands on the neck, West Point ride, German leg ride, amplitude (5-point) throws, and the Gabori/alligator roll (OAWA bans it Tyke to Bantam). [Ontario Amateur Combative Sport Rules: Wrestling - Part IV Competition requirements (eff. Jan 1 2025)](https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-wrestling/part-iv-competition-requirements)

### Banned moves - youngest

- (Tykes 7-8 / Novice 9-10) Tykes and Novice: no head-and-arm throw, no Gabori/rolling head-and-arm, no repeated tilts without reset; pins must be completed within 15 s; Novice/Kids/Bantam also may not lock hands in a double underhook or use reverse leg rides. [Ontario Amateur Combative Sport Rules: Wrestling - Part IV Competition requirements (eff. Jan 1 2025)](https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-wrestling/part-iv-competition-requirements)

### General illegal holds

- (All ages) Illegal at all levels: throat holds, twisting arms beyond 90 degrees, holding head/neck with two hands or any strangulation, chancery holds, and stretching the spinal column. [Ontario Amateur Combative Sport Rules: Wrestling - Part IV Competition requirements (eff. Jan 1 2025)](https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-wrestling/part-iv-competition-requirements)

### Referee safety principle

- (Youth) For young wrestlers officials should stop the action immediately if there is any doubt about safety, even when the move is legal: 'Safety must be a key goal of youth wrestling.' [Ontario Amateur Combative Sport Rules: Wrestling - Part IV Competition requirements (eff. Jan 1 2025)](https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-wrestling/part-iv-competition-requirements)

### Match length (youth)

- (Tykes to U17) Tykes/Novice and U15/U17: two 2-minute periods with 30-second break (some local youth events use 2 x 1.5 min). [Ontario Amateur Combative Sport Rules: Wrestling - Part IV Competition requirements (eff. Jan 1 2025)](https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-wrestling/part-iv-competition-requirements)

### Safe falling before throws

- (Teens (school standard)) Ontario school safety standard: throwing permitted only when break-fall/roll techniques are well established; skills taught in progression; partners of similar weight, strength and ability; constant visual supervision when first teaching holds/releases. [Ophea Ontario Physical Activity Safety Standards - Wrestling, Secondary Interschool 2023](https://safety.ophea.net/secondary/interschool/wrestling)

### Elementary-age wrestling

- (Elementary school age) Ophea elementary curricular wrestling (2025): no throws; direct supervision during initial instruction of holds and releases; wrestling-approved mats with no gaps; no contact for students with communicable skin conditions. [Ophea Ontario Physical Activity Safety Standards - Wrestling, Elementary Curricular 2025](https://safety.ophea.net/print/pdf/558)

### Kids no-gi legal submissions (Ontario)

- (7-17) Ontario grappling rules (Ontario Grappling Association), no-gi youth: straight armbar from 7, kimura/americana from 9, rear naked choke and triangle from 11; arm triangle, guillotine, Ezekiel, bow-and-arrow, omoplata, straight foot lock, gogoplata only from 16-17. [Ontario Amateur Combative Sport Rules: Grappling (Ontario Grappling Association) - Part IV](https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-grappling/part-iv-competition-requirements)

### Never-legal grappling techniques

- (All ages/belts) Not allowed at any age or belt in Ontario grappling: bicep lock, neck crank, twister, scissor takedown, full suplex, slam, striking, small-joint manipulation. In no-gi, heel hooks and calf slicers are allowed only at brown/black/elite. [Ontario Amateur Combative Sport Rules: Grappling (Ontario Grappling Association) - Part IV](https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-grappling/part-iv-competition-requirements)

### Kids jiu-jitsu (newaza) bans

- (4-15) Ontario Newaza Jiu Jitsu (Ontario Jiu Jitsu Association): for all kids, no arm triangle, Ezekiel, guillotine, neck crank, cervical locks, omoplata/gogoplata, wrist locks, leg attacks (except leg-stretch at 12-15), bicep slicers, scissor takedowns, jump-guard attacks or head-and-arm throws. [Ontario Amateur Combative Sport Rules: Newaza Jiu Jitsu (Ontario Jiu Jitsu Association) - Part IV](https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-newaza-jiu-jitsu/part-iv-competition-requirements)

### IBJJF kids/teen illegal techniques

- (4-12 and 13-15) IBJJF table (2024): for ages 4-15 illegal: Ezekiel, frontal guillotine, arm triangle, triangle pulling the head, omoplata, straight foot lock, leg-splitting submissions, wrist lock, bicep/calf slicer, kneebar, toe hold, heel hook; slams, knee reaping, scissor takedowns and spinal locks are illegal for everyone. [IBJJF illegal-techniques table as reproduced on a PBJJF Smoothcomp event page (IBJJF poster dated 2024-01-01)](https://pbjjf.smoothcomp.com/en/event/12912/page/30304/technical-fouls-illegal-moves)

### Youth MMA (Ontario)

- (12-18) Ontario amateur MMA categories: Youth C 12-13, Youth B 14-15, Youth A 16-18, Senior 19+; all head and face strikes are prohibited for Youth A, B and C. [Ontario Amateur Combative Sport Rules: Mixed Martial Arts - Part III Technical sport rules](https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-iii-technical-sport-rules)

### Youth MMA banned techniques

- (12-15) Youth B (14-15) bans knee strikes, grounded striking, bicep/calf crushes, wristlocks, kneebars, omoplata, slams, standing guillotines, up-kicks and stomps; Youth C (12-13) adds no grounded strikes of any kind, no guillotines and no leg submissions. [Ontario Amateur Combative Sport Rules: Mixed Martial Arts - Part III Technical sport rules](https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-iii-technical-sport-rules)

### MMA fouls at all levels

- (All) Ontario amateur MMA bans elbow/forearm strikes, neck cranks, heel hooks, toe holds and spine locks at every level; Pre-Novice also bans head strikes on a grounded opponent, suplexes, slams and shoulder throws. [Ontario Amateur Combative Sport Rules: Mixed Martial Arts - Part III Technical sport rules](https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-iii-technical-sport-rules)

### MMA equipment and medical

- (All) Amateur MMA: soft neoprene shin guards required, gloves at least 6 oz, no headgear; annual physical by an Ontario-licensed physician plus pre-bout medical; contestants sign a Concussion Code of Conduct. [Ontario Amateur Combative Sport Rules: Mixed Martial Arts - Part I General (updated Jun 2025)](https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts/part-i-general)

### Who sanctions amateur MMA

- (All) All amateur MMA contests or exhibitions in Ontario must be sanctioned by the Ontario Athletics Commission (no PSO is listed for MMA). [Ontario Amateur Combative Sport Rules: Mixed Martial Arts (overview)](https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-mixed-martial-arts)

### Kick light / light contact kickboxing

- (7-15 and adults) Kickboxing Ontario light-contact categories: Cadet 7-9, Junior A 10-12, Junior B 13-15; 2 x 2-min rounds with 1-min rest; warnings for excessive contact; spinning backfist, knees, throws and grappling are illegal. [Ontario Amateur Combative Sport Rules: Kickboxing - Kick Light and Light Contact, Part IV](https://www.ontario.ca/document/amateur-combative-sport-rules-kickboxing-kick-light-and-light-contact/part-iv-competition)

### Contests need sanction

- (All) Amateur combative contests/exhibitions in Ontario are governed by the Combative Sports Act, 2019; they need an OAC permit or must be held/sanctioned by a ministry-recognized PSO (Boxing Ontario, OAWA, OGA, OJJA, Kickboxing Ontario, etc.). [Ontario - Amateur combative sports events (updated Sep 2025)](https://www.ontario.ca/page/amateur-combative-sports)

### Training vs contests

- (All) Ontario's guidance covers contests and exhibitions; events with only light contact are among those that do not need a permit. Ontario's 2022 fact sheet said training, skills development and non-contact demonstrations are not prize fights. [Ontario - Amateur combative sports events (updated Sep 2025)](https://www.ontario.ca/page/amateur-combative-sports)

### Light contact in school combatives

- (School-age) Ophea intramural combatives/wrestling standards now permit only 'light contact' activities, using the light-contact definition in Ontario Regulation 507/24 (controlled techniques not intended or expected to cause harm). [Ophea - Wrestling and Combatives Safety Standards Updates](https://safety.ophea.net/wrestling-and-combatives-safety-standards-updates)

### Rowan's Law - who is a sport organization

- (Under 26) Rowan's Law covers 'a person or entity that carries out, for profit or otherwise, a prescribed activity' in connection with amateur competitive sport, so private for-profit gyms can be covered. [Legislative Assembly of Ontario - Bill 193, Rowan's Law (Concussion Safety), 2018](https://www.ola.org/en/legislative-business/bills/parliament-41/session-2/bill-193/acts-affected)

### Rowan's Law - prescribed activities

- (Under 26) The prescribed activities include training athletes, conducting practices and organizing competitions; the sports list is in O. Reg. 161/19 (not verified here because e-Laws would not load). [Canadian Lawyer - Rowan's Law: all about competitive sports, concussions, and the children (Dec 2024)](https://www.canadianlawyermag.com/resources/legal-education/rowans-law-all-about-competitive-sports-concussions-and-the-children/390419)

### Rowan's Law - awareness resources

- (Athletes under 26; parents of under-18s) Before registering, athletes under 26 and parents of athletes under 18 must confirm they reviewed a Concussion Awareness Resource within the previous 12 months; coaches, team trainers and officials confirm every year. [Ontario - Rowan's Law: information for sport organizations and schools (updated Sep 2026)](https://www.ontario.ca/page/rowans-law-information-sports-organizations-and-schools)

### Rowan's Law - Concussion Code of Conduct

- (Under 26) Sport organizations must have a Concussion Code of Conduct (since July 1, 2019) that athletes under 26, parents of under-18s, coaches and team trainers confirm every year; Ontario provides sample codes. [Ontario - Rowan's Law concussion awareness resources (updated Jan 2026)](https://www.ontario.ca/page/rowans-law-concussion-awareness-resources)

### Rowan's Law - removal and return protocols

- (Under 26) Sport organizations must have Removal-from-Sport and Return-to-Sport protocols (since Jan 1, 2022): immediate removal from training, practice or competition, parents told for under-18s, and no return until the return-to-sport process is complete. [Ontario - Rowan's Law: concussion safety (updated Aug 2026)](https://www.ontario.ca/page/rowans-law-concussion-safety)

### Designated persons

- (All) Each practice, training or competition needs a designated person who can remove athletes; they must have reviewed the awareness resources in the past 12 months; they do not diagnose. CAO recommends NCCP Making Headway. [Coaches Association of Ontario - FAQ: You are the Designated Person under Rowan's Law (Oct 2024)](https://www.coachesontario.ca/programs-resources/concussion/faq/)

### Medical clearance

- (All) Only physicians and nurse practitioners can medically clear an athlete to return to amateur competitive sport. [Ontario - Rowan's Law: information for sport organizations and schools (updated Sep 2026)](https://www.ontario.ca/page/rowans-law-information-sports-organizations-and-schools)

### Age-specific e-booklets

- (10 and under / 11-14 / 15+) Ontario publishes concussion e-booklets for ages 10 and under, 11-14, and 15 and up, plus sample codes of conduct and an optional confirmation receipt form. [Ontario - Rowan's Law concussion awareness resources (updated Jan 2026)](https://www.ontario.ca/page/rowans-law-concussion-awareness-resources)

### Recreational programs scope

- (All) Coaches Association of Ontario: recreational providers must decide for themselves whether their programs fall within Rowan's Law 'prescribed activity'; drop-in programs are not specifically addressed. [Coaches Association of Ontario - FAQ: You are the Designated Person under Rowan's Law (Oct 2024)](https://www.coachesontario.ca/programs-resources/concussion/faq/)

### Boxing Ontario concussion policy

- (All BO members) Boxing Ontario's Concussion Policy applies to all BO activities including practices and training at member clubs, and is intended to comply with Rowan's Law; it is based on the 2017 consensus and still contains an unfilled designated-person placeholder. [Boxing Ontario - Concussion Policy and Code (uploaded 2025-04)](https://boxingontario.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Boxing-Ontario-Concussion-Policy-and-Code.pdf)

### Return-to-sport steps (2024)

- (All) Parachute 2024: 6 steps, each at least 24 h (relative rest first 24-48 h, light then moderate aerobic, sport-specific without head-impact risk, non-contact drills, full-contact practice, return to sport); mild brief symptom increase is OK in steps 1-3. [Parachute - Return-to-Sport and Return-to-School Protocols (2024, Canadian Guideline 2nd ed.)](https://pedsconcussion.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Parachute-Return-to-Sport-and-School-Protocols-2024.pdf)

### Medical clearance timing (2024)

- (All) Parachute 2024 says the athlete gives the coach a Medical Clearance Letter before progressing to step 4 and must have returned to full-time school first; Boxing Ontario's older policy places clearance after stage 5. [Parachute - Return-to-Sport and Return-to-School Protocols (2024, Canadian Guideline 2nd ed.)](https://pedsconcussion.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Parachute-Return-to-Sport-and-School-Protocols-2024.pdf)

### Pre-season education

- (All) Canadian Guideline (2024): all stakeholders review and sign a pre-season concussion education sheet; a standardized Medical Clearance Letter from an MD or NP is required before full-contact practice and games. [Parachute - Canadian Guideline on Concussion in Sport, 2nd ed. (Mar 2024)](https://parachute.ca/en/professional-resource/concussion-collection/canadian-guideline-on-concussion-in-sport/)

### Rule of Two

- (Minors) Responsible Coaching Movement: all interactions and communications with participants are open, observable and justifiable, with two responsible adults (coach, parent, staff or screened volunteer) present; exceptions only in emergencies. [Coaching Association of Canada - Responsible Coaching Movement](https://coach.ca/responsible-coaching-movement)

### Rule of Two (Boxing Ontario)

- (Minors) Boxing Ontario: an athlete must never be alone one-on-one with an unrelated Person in Authority; where full implementation isn't possible, keep training environments visible and accessible. [Boxing Ontario - UCCMS Safe Sport Policy](https://boxingontario.com/?p=25666)

### RCM three pillars

- (Coaches) The Responsible Coaching Movement has three parts: Rule of Two, background screening (references, interviews, police checks repeated at intervals) and ethics training. [CCES - Responsible Coaching Movement](https://cces.ca/rcm)

### Electronic communication

- (Minors) Example NSO practice: eliminate one-to-one electronic messaging with participants; send communications to the group and/or include parents; another adult present when a participant rides in a coach's vehicle. [Skate Canada - Rule of Two recommendations (Dec 2024; example NSO implementation)](https://skatecanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Rule-of-Two-EN.pdf)

### UCCMS boundaries

[Universal Code of Conduct to Prevent and Address Maltreatment in Sport (UCCMS) v7.0, eff. Apr 1 2025](https://sportintegrity.ca/sites/default/files/content/docs/2025-01/CCES-UCCMS-Final-E.pdf)

### Duty to report

- (Adults) UCCMS: adult participants must report prohibited behaviour they know of or ought to have known of; Boxing Ontario routes complaints to its discipline office (within 21 days) and suspected child abuse to local authorities. [Universal Code of Conduct to Prevent and Address Maltreatment in Sport (UCCMS) v7.0, eff. Apr 1 2025](https://sportintegrity.ca/sites/default/files/content/docs/2025-01/CCES-UCCMS-Final-E.pdf)

### Neuromuscular warm-up

- (Youth) NMT warm-ups typically take 10-15 minutes and combine aerobic, balance, strength and agility exercises; they reduce injury rates by up to about 60%, with adherence of 1.5+ sessions/week linked to benefit. [Lutz et al., BJSM 58(11):615 - Best practices for NMT injury-prevention warm-ups in youth team sport (systematic review)](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/58/11/615)

### FIFA 11+ Kids evidence

- (7-13) A roughly 20-minute kids warm-up done at least once and preferably twice a week cut injuries by 48% and severe injuries by up to 74% in a 3,900-child trial (soccer; principles transferable but untested in combat sports). [University of Basel press release on FIFA 11+ Kids trial (Jan 2018)](https://idw-online.de/de/news687360)

### Daily activity and sleep

- (5-17) Canadian 24-Hour Guidelines: at least 60 min/day of moderate-to-vigorous activity; vigorous plus muscle- and bone-strengthening at least 3 days/week; sleep 9-11 h (5-13) or 8-10 h (14-17); no more than 2 h/day recreational screen time. [CSEP - Canadian 24-Hour Movement Guidelines for Children and Youth (5-17)](https://csepguidelines.ca/guidelines/children-youth/)

### Training volume limits

- (Youth) NATA: organized sport hours per week should not exceed the athlete's age; at least 2 days off per week; no more than 8 months/year in one sport; one team per season; delay specialization. [NATA - Sport Specialization Recommendations for Adolescent and Young Athletes (Oct 2019)](https://www.nata.org/sites/default/files/youth_sports_specialization_recommendations.pdf)

### Overtraining and burnout

- (Youth) AAP (2024): avoid overtraining and overscheduling, keep workouts fun with age-appropriate games, and protect free play time; overtraining shows as falling performance, persistent fatigue, poor sleep and mood changes. [AAP - Overuse Injuries, Overtraining, and Burnout in Young Athletes (Pediatrics, Feb 2024) news summary](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/news/Pages/AAP-calls-out-causes-of-injury-overtraining-and-burnout-in-youth-sports.aspx)

### LTD general framework

- (Children) Sport for Life: FUNdamentals (boys 6-9, girls 6-8) is about 75% general activity and 25% sport-specific, built on agility, balance, coordination and speed; Learn to Train is about 70% training and 30% competition-related activity; progress by development, not just age. [Sport for Life - The First Three Stages of Long-Term Development (Dec 2024)](https://sportforlife.ca/blog/building-a-solid-foundation-the-first-three-stages-of-long-term-development-in-sport-and-physical-activity/)

### Women starting boxing later

- (Teens and adult women) Boxing Canada: most women join boxing at 15-22, but they must still go through all the basic stages progressively; girls with limited sport backgrounds should focus on basic boxing skills and general athletic development. [Boxing Canada (CABA) Long-Term Athlete Development guide (hosted by Boxing Ontario)](https://boxingontario.com/wp-content/uploads/Boxing-Canada-LTAD.pdf)

### Women's sparring opportunities

- (Women (novice)) Boxing Ontario's Female Development Committee runs camps with controlled, pre-matched sparring supervised by experienced coaches; clubs can host all-female sparring sessions and join the 'Female Sparring in Ontario' list. [Boxing Ontario - Female Development Committee camp news (IWD 2026)](https://boxingontario.com/iwd-2026-give-to-gain-boxing-ontarios-female-development-committee-hosts-female-development-camp-at-tnt-boxing/)

### Girls' retention

- (Girls 6-18) Rally Report 2024: more than 1 in 5 girls leave sport during adolescence, and 7 in 10 girls say sport improves their mental health. [Canadian Women & Sport - Rally Report 2024](https://womenandsport.ca/rally-2024/)

### Women's protective equipment (MMA)

- (Women) Ontario amateur MMA rules: a groin or breast protector is optional for women, while men must wear a foul-proof groin protector (for boxing, see the Ontario boxing equipment item). ### Long-term athlete development

- (0-6) Active Start (ages 0-6): children need daily active play with an emphasis on fun, a wide variety of equipment, non-competitive games and safe chances to explore risk and limits. Fundamental movement skills are learned through play. [Sport for Life, Active Start](https://sportforlife.ca/stages/active-start/)

## Notes by class

**Kids Boxing (Wed 5:15-6:15, kids from about 4)**
- Kids under 6 are in Sport for Life's Active Start stage (0-6): daily active play, fun first, learning to move and link movements together through play [Sport for Life, Active Start](https://sportforlife.ca/stages/active-start/).
- Most kids aged 6-9 are in FUNdamentals: fun games built on agility, balance, coordination and speed, with about 75% general movement and 25% boxing [Sport for Life; Boxing Canada LTAD].
- A 60-minute session fits Boxing Canada's 45-75 min 'introduction to boxing' guidance (1-2 sessions per week for this stage) [Boxing Canada LTAD].
- No maximal or near-maximal strength work; adapt equipment (gloves, bags, pads) to the child's size [Boxing Canada LTAD].
- Keep speed efforts under 5 seconds with full recovery, and do them early, after the warm-up [Boxing Canada LTAD].
- Keep it non-contact for 6-8s. Boxing Canada introduces coach-led simulated bouts, conditioned sparring and shadow boxing progressively only from Learn to Box (boys 9+, girls 8+) [Boxing Canada LTAD].
- Fun Box is the competition format for 6-10s (Boxing Ontario) or 6-12s (Ontario ruleset): 3 x 1 min, cheek-protector headgear allowed, heavy blows discouraged [Boxing Ontario Match Guidelines 2025; Ontario Boxing Rules Part V; Boxing BC FUNBox].
- Look at Boxing Ontario's non-contact BOX-ON! format (ages 8-16) as a template [Boxing Ontario BOX-ON! article].
- Parents confirm the Ontario concussion e-booklet for ages 10 and under before registration [Rowan's Law resources].
- Apply the Rule of Two: two responsible adults present and no coach alone one-on-one with a child [CAC RCM; Boxing Ontario Safe Sport].
- Be ready for parents to raise the Canadian Paediatric Society's 2011 view against youth boxing; a non-contact format answers most of it [CPS].

**Wrestling (Mon 3:20-4:00, kids)**
- Group by OAWA age bands: Tykes 7-8, Novice 9-10, Kids 11-12, with BBG's 6-year-olds in their own group or with the Tykes [OAWA Youth Wrestling Rules].
- Keep it play-based for under-10s: structured play, play-wrestling, tumbling, modified rules [Wrestling Canada LTAD Manual].
- For 10-12s use games and simulations, and avoid complex tactics, highly repetitive drills and high-intensity conditioning [Wrestling Canada LTAD Manual].
- Teach breakfalls and rolls first; only teach throws once falling is well established, and for elementary-age groups keep it to no throws [Ophea Secondary 2023; Ophea Elementary 2025].
- Never teach or allow slams, back-bending throws, full or 3/4 nelson, two hands on the neck, chancery/throat holds, arm twists beyond 90 degrees, West Point ride, German leg ride or Gabori; also no head-and-arm throw for Tykes/Novice [Ontario Wrestling Rules Part IV; OAWA].
- Stop any live action when in doubt about safety, even if it is legal [Ontario Wrestling Rules Part IV].
- Keep live goes short: youth matches are 2 x 2 min with a 30-second break [Ontario Wrestling Rules Part IV].
- Match partners by weight, strength and ability, and give direct supervision while first teaching holds and releases [Ophea].
- Use wrestling-approved mats with no gaps, clean them before every session, and keep anyone with a skin infection off contact drills [Ophea Elementary 2025].
- Plan water breaks and watch heat, because children regulate temperature poorly [Wrestling Canada LTAD Manual].
- Fit a 10-15 min neuromuscular warm-up into the 40-minute slot (balance, landing, agility, strength) [Lutz et al. BJSM].
