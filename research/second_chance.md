# Second Chance: setup and funding research

Researched October 20, 2026. This is factual research, not legal or accounting advice. **[LAWYER]** and **[ACCOUNTANT]** mark items to take to a professional.

## 1. Incorporation: Ontario (ONCA) or federal (CNCA)
- **Start here:** search the Ontario Business Registry, Corporations Canada and CRA's charity list by full legal name to see where the filing stands. [https://nonprofitlaw.cleo.on.ca/start-a-nonprofit/how-do-we-find-our-nonprofits-current-status/]
- **ONCA costs:** $155 to incorporate (5 business days online), and the annual return is $0. [https://www.ontario.ca/page/companies-service-standards-and-fees] A NUANS name search costs $22 to $40. [https://nonprofitlaw.cleo.on.ca/start-a-nonprofit/how-do-we-incorporate/]
- **ONCA directors:** at least 3. In a public benefit corporation, which includes charities, no more than one-third can be employees. [https://www.ontario.ca/page/not-profit-corporations-act-2010-transition-considerations]
- **ONCA by-law:** if none is passed within 60 days of incorporation, a standard organizational by-law applies automatically. [same page] CLEO's Bylaw Builder is free. [https://nonprofitlaw.cleo.on.ca/transition-to-onca/update-governing-documents/cleos-bylaw-builder/]
- **CNCA costs:** $200 online, and the annual return is $12. [https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2011-223/page-5.html]
- **CNCA directors:** 1 minimum, or 3 for a soliciting corporation (over $10,000 a year from public sources). [https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2021/09/cnca-vs-onca-similar-but-not-the-same]
- **CNCA filings:** by-laws are filed with Corporations Canada, and the corporation must also register in Ontario. [https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/corporations-canada/en/node/35]
- **Which fits:** CLEO says "One is not better than the other." ONCA needs an Ontario address and no financial filings with government. [https://nonprofitlaw.cleo.on.ca/start-a-nonprofit/is-it-better-to-incorporate-under-onca-or-cnca/] *My inference:* for a Bancroft-only group, ONCA is cheaper and needs only one registration.
- **Audit relief for charities under ONCA:** with revenue under $100,000, an 80% members' vote each year waives both audit and review. Under $500,000, a review engagement can replace an audit. [https://nonprofitlaw.cleo.on.ca/transition-to-onca/understand-the-law/financial-reviews-and-audits/] **[ACCOUNTANT]**

## 2. CRA charitable registration
- **The purpose is recognized.** CRA Summary Policy CSP-R16, *Social rehabilitation*, says organizations "established for social rehabilitation (for example, rehabilitation of prisoners)" can qualify under "other purposes beneficial to the community." [https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/policies-guidance/summary-policy-r16-social-rehabilitation.html]
- **Employment angle:** CRA Guidance CG-014, *Community economic development activities and charitable registration*, accepts "advancing education by providing employment-related training" and relieving the unemployment of people experiencing poverty. It doesn't mention criminal records, and CRA is updating it. [https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/policies-guidance/community-economic-development-activities-charitable-registration-014.html]
  - On-the-job training must "provide training, not jobs."
  - Training "must not be limited to a specific employer."
- **Write the purposes carefully.** "Anyone who needs a fresh start" is broad. CRA has a short guide to writing purposes. [https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/registering-charitable-qualified-donee-status/apply-become-registered-charity/establishing/charitable-purposes/short-guide-how-to-write-charitable-purpose.html] **[LAWYER]**
- **Board:** more than 50% of directors must deal with each other at arm's length, or the charity is classed as a private foundation. [https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/registering-charitable-qualified-donee-status/apply-become-registered-charity/establishing/types-registered-charities-designations.html] People related "by blood, marriage or common law partnership, or adoption," or acting "in concert," are not at arm's length. [https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/charities-giving-glossary.html]
- **How to apply:** online in My Business Account, with certified governing documents, a director list and a full description of activities. [https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/registering-charitable-qualified-donee-status/apply-become-registered-charity/apply/submit-application.html]
- **Timing:** CRA aims to decide "within nine months of receiving a complete application," 80% of the time. [https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/service-standards-cra/service-standards-2025-26.html]
- **2025–26 denial reasons:** non-charitable activities, too little detail on activities, and no public benefit. 79.2% of applicants were registered. [https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/about-charities-directorate/report-on-charities-program/report-on-charities-program-2025-2026.html]
- **Private benefit:** acceptable only as "a minor and incidental by-product." [https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/policies-guidance/policy-statement-024-guidelines-registering-a-charity-meeting-public-benefit-test.html]
- **Undue benefit:** giving one to a non-arm's-length person costs a penalty of 105% of its value. [https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/policies-guidance/summary-policy-u02-undue-benefits.html]
- **Paying a director's business (Ontario):** this needs O. Reg. 4/01 compliance, including written approval from every other director and at least 4 voting directors besides the conflicted one. [https://millerthomson.com/fr/perspectives-juridiques/impact-social-fr/payments-to-directors-and-connected-persons-key-info-for-ontario-charitable-corporations]
- **Conflicts under ONCA:** a conflicted director discloses, leaves the discussion and doesn't vote. [https://nonprofitlaw.cleo.on.ca/run-a-nonprofit/conflicts-of-interest/]
- **Fight-night volunteering:** I found **no** CRA guidance on this exact case. If CCCL is the only placement, it gets free labour, which risks failing CG-014's "specific employer" test and the private-benefit test. **[LAWYER]**

## 5. Law and good practice
- **Human Rights Code:** in employment, nobody can be refused for a "record of offences" (pardoned federal or provincial convictions) unless a clean record is "a reasonable and bona fide qualification." [https://www.ohrc.on.ca/en/your-rights/code-grounds/record-offences]
- **Police checks come in 3 types:** criminal record; criminal record and judicial matters; and vulnerable sector. [https://www.ontario.ca/policerecordchecks]
  - A vulnerable sector check is only for people "in a position of trust or authority over children" (under 18) or other vulnerable persons.
  - The person must consent. Results go to them first, and they decide whether to release them.
- **OPP costs:** $41 a check; volunteer checks are free. [https://www.opp.ca/index.php?id=147]
- **Screening:** the police check is step 7 of Volunteer Canada's 10. [https://volunteer.ca/screening/]
- **Rule of Two:** "two responsible adults… are present with a participant," and the environment is "open, observable and justifiable." [https://coach.ca/rule-two]
- **A reasonable policy** (my synthesis; **[LAWYER]** to review):
  - Place participants in roles with no authority over minors, such as setup, adult ticketing, and security support alongside staff.
  - Never leave a participant alone with a youth athlete.
  - Run a vulnerable sector check for any role with youth contact.
  - Decide each person individually, not with blanket bans.
- **Privacy:** PIPEDA usually doesn't apply to nonprofits, but its principles are recommended. [https://priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/02_05_d_19] Keep record details out of shared Sheets and Drive. Log only "cleared / role-limited," the date and written consent.

## 6. First-year budget items
- **Incorporation:** ONCA $155 plus a $22–$40 NUANS search.
- **CRA application:** no fee listed. The T3010 is due 6 months after year-end. [https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/charities/operating-a-registered-charity/t3010-charity-return-overview.html]
- **Police checks:** $0 for volunteers, $41 for staff.
- **Insurance:** ONN's program (HUB International) offers D&O, liability, event and abuse coverage. Prices require a quote. [https://theonn.ca/services/nonprofit-insurance/]

## Recommended first 90 days
1. Confirm the registry status; choose ONCA if nothing is filed.
2. Fix the board: 3+ directors, more than half at arm's length.
3. Pass a by-law within 60 days and a conflict policy. Cody discloses his BBG and CCCL interests.
4. Have a lawyer draft purposes using the CSP-R16 and CG-014 wording.
5. Open a bank account and keep books from day one (OTF needs a year of statements).
6. Apply for Canada Summer Jobs after Oct 27, 2026.
7. Ask the four nearest partners about leading a United Way application (due Dec 11) or an OTF collaboration.
8. Adopt screening, Rule of Two and privacy policies, and get insurance quotes before the first event.
9. Add placement hosts beyond CCCL.
10. Submit a complete CRA application.
